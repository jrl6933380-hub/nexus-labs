# Nexus ad site lineup

Goal: show a broad range of believable, individually designed business sites.
Each concept should have a recognizable business, a primary customer task and a
visual identity that remains distinct in a short ad or phone-screen recording.
Concepts demonstrate design possibilities; they do not imply real clients,
completed orders, live integrations or customer results.

## Suggested first six

1. Tattoo atelier: Stillmark, the approved independent Codex direction. Show the
   artwork, comfortable mobile typography and consultation reveal.
2. Food truck: bold street-menu graphics, a real-feeling menu board, prominent
   sample stop information and pickup enquiry. Never imply live tracking.
3. Landscaper: calm outdoor imagery, a service-area story and seasonal quote flow.
   Any before/after pair must be labeled concept, not claimed client results.
4. Photographer: a full portfolio-led composition, contact sheets and quiet type.
   Generated images must be marked illustrative, not actual client sessions.
5. Auto repair: practical, readable service navigation, vehicle intake and clear
   repair enquiry. Avoid invented credentials, prices or promises.
6. Custom bakery: warm tactile product imagery, occasion selection and a visual
   cake enquiry. Do not imply real ordering or payment until connected.

## Ad formats to explore after the sites exist

- One business, one transformation: a brief becomes a polished concept, followed
  by one actual local interaction. Show only what the current tools can do.
- Same request, different businesses: cut between distinctly composed sites to
  show variety. Do not claim that one template fits every business.
- The owner's point of view: show what a customer would see and what a connected
  enquiry could provide. Label unconnected destinations as a planned integration.
- Phone-first walkthrough: tap the menu, read the work, open a consultation.
- "Which business is yours?" montage: a quick lineup followed by the real Nexus
  waitlist/build request destination once its URL is confirmed.

Build each site independently. Share reliable accessibility and form mechanics,
not a mandatory hero/services/gallery skeleton. Use customer-provided assets when
available; generated assets illustrate a concept and must not masquerade as proof.
